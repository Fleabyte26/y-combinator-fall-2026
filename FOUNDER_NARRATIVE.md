1. Y Combinator Founder Narrative ("Why us / Why this team?")
Prompt: What is your domain expertise, and why are you uniquely qualified to build this?
I have spent 25 years inside enterprise physical security technology and industrial deployments across the Americas. I have designed, integrated, and scaled video and telemetry systems for distributed commercial infrastructure, border environments, critical logistics, and enterprise facilities.

Most founders entering this space look at physical security through a Silicon Valley consumer lens: they assume continuous gigabit bandwidth, cheap cloud compute, and forgiving error margins. I know the ground reality: enterprise networks are bandwidth-starved, cellular drops constantly, camera streams saturate uplinks, and security directors turn off systems that produce false alarms.

I am building this because I have lived the pain of the edge bandwidth bottleneck for two and a half decades. I understand the exact operational failure modes of enterprise surveillance, the economics of hardware and cellular pipelines, and the technical architecture required to extract mathematical ground truth without dumping raw pixels into the cloud.

2. In-Q-Tel (IQT) Operational Fit & Tactical Grounding
Operational Track Record
Quarter-Century of Field Deployment: 25 years navigating complex, distributed enterprise physical security networks, harsh hardware environments, and multi-sensor integration.

Regional Deployment Realities: Extensive experience managing enterprise security technology infrastructure across volatile, infrastructure-constrained regions in Latin America and the Caribbean.

Systems Engineering Grounding: Direct operational fluency in local virtualized environments, network packet analysis (Wireshark), defensive security controls, and embedded edge compute constraints.

The IQT Thesis Fit
Silicon Valley VCs frequently confuse consumer SaaS with operational edge computing. In-Q-Tel requires systems that survive contested logistics, electronic warfare, and degraded networks.

My entire career has been focused on enterprise environments where hardware must function when the outside world drops off. This platform was conceived not in a cloud testbed, but from the physical reality of mission-critical facilities that cannot afford connectivity-dependent architectures.
